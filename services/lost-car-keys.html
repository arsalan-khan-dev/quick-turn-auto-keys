Lost Your Only Key?

Here's what usually happens next

If you've lost the only key to your vehicle, the process is a little different from cutting a spare from an existing key. Depending on the vehicle and its security system, a replacement may still be possible, but it typically involves confirming ownership and, in many cases, programming a new key directly to the vehicle.

If you do still have a spare key anywhere � at home, with a family member, or in another bag or coat � let us know, as this can sometimes make the process quicker and more straightforward.

Before We Attend

A few details that help us prepare

Having the following ready when you call or request a quote helps us understand your situation and prepare the right equipment for your vehicle.

Vehicle Details

Make, model, year and registration number help us identify the key system your vehicle uses.

Proof of Ownership

Documents such as your V5C logbook, insurance or purchase paperwork may be required for security purposes.

Your Current Location

Whether you're at home, at work or stranded somewhere unfamiliar, we'll need an accurate location to attend.

Any Remaining Keys

Let us know if you have a spare key, even a non-working or damaged one � it can be useful information for the technician.

Supported Key Types

We work with a range of vehicle key systems

Standard Keys

Older mechanical keys can often be replaced by accurately cutting to match your vehicle's lock.

Transponder Keys

These require the chip to be programmed to your vehicle's immobiliser as well as being cut correctly.

Remote Fobs

Combine a physical key with remote buttons � replacement usually involves both cutting and programming.

Smart & Keyless Keys

Proximity and push-button start systems require vehicle-specific programming equipment on-site.
